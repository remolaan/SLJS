type: precedent
case: Silva v Attorney-General (Hypothetical, illustrative) [2021] SLCA 0456

Facts:
The accused was convicted of theft of movable property contrary to section
367 of the Penal Code. At trial the prosecution relied on circumstantial
evidence and a confession. The accused appealed, arguing the confession was
involuntary and that the circumstantial evidence did not exclude every
reasonable hypothesis consistent with innocence.

Issues:
Whether the confession was admissible and voluntary; whether circumstantial
evidence sufficed to prove the dishonest taking beyond reasonable doubt.

Reasoning:
The Court reiterated that a confession must be voluntary to be admissible
under the Evidence Ordinance. Circumstantial evidence may found a
conviction only where it is consistent with guilt and inconsistent with any
reasonable hypothesis of innocence. The prosecution bears the burden
throughout.

Held:
Appeal allowed; conviction and sentence set aside on the ground that the
confession was not shown to be voluntary and the remaining evidence did not
exclude reasonable doubt.
