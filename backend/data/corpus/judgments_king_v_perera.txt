type: precedent
case: King v Perera (Hypothetical, illustrative) [2024] SLHC 0123

Facts:
The accused was charged with voluntarily causing grievous hurt contrary to
section 324 of the Penal Code following an altercation in a marketplace.
The victim suffered a fracture of the arm. The accused claimed the injury
resulted from a sudden quarrel and that he acted without premeditation and
in the heat of the moment.

Issues:
Whether the injury amounted to grievous hurt within section 322; whether
the sudden quarrel negated the intent required for the aggravated offence.

Reasoning:
The Court held that a fracture of a bone constitutes grievous hurt under
section 322. However, where the harm is inflicted in the course of a sudden
fight without premeditation, the case may fall within the mitigating
framework, and the court may temper the sentence accordingly. The burden
remains on the prosecution to establish the elements of the offence beyond
reasonable doubt.

Held:
Conviction under section 324 sustained, but the custodial sentence was
reduced in light of provocation and the absence of premeditation.
